Doctrine · How We Work

Place first.
Paper second.
Always in the open.

We are not in a hurry, and we are not loud about it. Here is the whole method — what we do, what we refuse to do, and what we publish for anyone to reuse.

Four principles,
enforced daily

/ 01

Place before product

No deck ships before the town is understood — its tracts, its county, its state program, its working people. If we cannot point at it on a map, we do not publish it.

/ 02

Public benefit by design

A public-benefit approach means the benefit is structural — local ownership, restored main streets, durable jobs — not a paragraph bolted onto a pitch.

/ 03

Open by default

Research, maps, checklists, and doctrine are published so that the next town — and the next operator — starts further ahead than we did.

/ 04

Stewardship over speed

We review before we publish, and we decline before we stretch. A smaller, truthful index beats a large, hopeful one — every single time.

What we do —
and what we don’t

/ WE DO

The work, plainly

  • → Review Opportunity Zone context for specific places and publish approximate reference points.
  • → Point residents to official state and federal resources for their own diligence.
  • → Issue one-time guide-access invoices for review, after approval — never automatically.
  • → Keep our method public so others can audit, reuse, and improve it.
  • → Say “we don’t know yet” when we don’t know yet.
/ WE DON’T

The lines, plainly

  • ✕ Take custody of your funds, securities, keys, or credentials.
  • ✕ Sell, or arrange the sale of, ownership interests of any kind on this site.
  • ✕ Declare any property eligible for an Opportunity Zone — tracts decide that.
  • ✕ Give tax, legal, or financial advice. We are not your fiduciary.
  • ✕ Publish a town page before review — flattery is not diligence.

The two ideas
behind the name

/ PUBLIC BENEFIT CORPORATION

For profit, on purpose

A public benefit corporation (PBC) is a for-profit company with a stated public-benefit purpose alongside its business goals. It is not a nonprofit. Formation rules depend on the state — see the Delaware PBC overview. PBC formation is a separate offering and is not included with guide access.

/ OPPORTUNITY ZONES

A tract, not a town

Opportunity Zones are low-income census tracts designated as eligible for certain investment incentives under federal law. Designations are made tract by tract — a ZIP code is not an Opportunity Zone, and original designations date to December 2019. Verify any address against the official program for your state and the U.S. Treasury’s Opportunity Zone resource.
