The Word Ledger · Plain Definitions

Glossary

Every term we use on this site, defined the way we would explain it to a neighbor. Educational only — not legal, tax, or financial advice.

Public Benefit Corporation (PBC)
A for-profit company whose charter states a public-benefit purpose alongside its business goals. Not a nonprofit. Formation rules vary by state — see the Delaware overview for the canonical example.
PBC Formation
The legal process of incorporating a public benefit corporation under state law. It is a separate service with its own terms — it is never bundled into guide access, and it is not included in the US$1,000.
Opportunity Zone
An economically distressed census tract designated as eligible for certain investment incentives under federal law. Designations are made tract by tract — a town, county, or ZIP code cannot be “an Opportunity Zone.”
Qualified Opportunity Fund
An investment vehicle organized to deploy capital into Opportunity Zone property. This site does not offer, broker, or solicit interests in any fund — that is between you and your advisors.
Census Tract
A small, permanent statistical geography defined by the U.S. Census Bureau — typically a few thousand residents. It, not the ZIP code, is the unit of Opportunity Zone designation.
ZIP Code / ZCTA
A postal delivery area. A ZIP Code Tabulation Area may overlap a tract, may not, and never decides eligibility. We always verify by tract.
Original Designation
The first slate of Qualified Opportunity Zone tracts, designated in 2018–2019. Original designation coverage reflects that era unless a state program states otherwise.
Reference Point
The approximate coordinate where a territory’s map marker and research center sit — a starting point for diligence, not a boundary or an eligibility claim.
Territory
Our word for a reviewed place in the index — a town, county, ZIP area, calling area, or city — each carrying context, coordinates, and official resources.
First Batch
The opening cohort of nine reviewed territories: Atlanta, Emeryville, Las Cruces, Logan, Orange, Rancho Santa Margarita, Salina, San Gabriel, and Westport.
Batch 2 / Batch 3
The second and third reviewed cohorts — thirteen and eight territories respectively — expanding the index state by state.
Town Page
A published, reviewed local page for a specific town or ZIP — the artifact residents receive when their request survives review.
Capital Formation
The assembly of money, labor, and organization into durable, productive local assets — buildings, operating businesses, and the institutions around them.
Real Assets
Physical, place-anchored value: land, buildings, infrastructure, and operating businesses. Contrast with purely paper or abstract instruments.
Open Source Capital Formation
Publishing the method — maps, checklists, doctrine — so the work itself is reusable and auditable, the way open source software is.
Bruno Agent
The local agent capability The Reconstruction Company is preparing with Manus for guided work in specific territories. Its purchase is guide access — a service, never ownership.
Guide Access
The one-time service of being guided through a territory’s context by the team — issued only by invoice after review and approval. Not an investment product.
Manus
The platform the founding team adopted in its first week of public release, where coordination, research, agent development, and team workflow now live.
Executive-Function Environment
An operating environment organized around planning, priorities, and follow-through — as opposed to a chat-room organized around conversation.
Stewardship
Holding other people’s trust lightly — review before publishing, refuse before stretching, and keep the ledger legible to anyone who asks.

Definitions are plain-language summaries written for residents and operators. Statutory text controls where this page and the law differ. For decisions that move money or sign documents, retain qualified counsel and your own diligence team.
